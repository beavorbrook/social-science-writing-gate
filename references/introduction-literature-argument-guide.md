# Introduction & Literature Argument Guide — v2.3.0

**Purpose.** Help an economics or environmental-economics manuscript pose a consequential answerable economic question, state its actual marginal contribution, and synthesize source-verified prior empirical findings. This is a **writing/independent-review reference**, not an econometric prescription, additional dependency, or replacement for the installed official literature validators. Use with existing `SKILL.md` and the approved manuscript.

## 1. Establish the economic object before drafting

Freeze a compact **Question–Answer–Contribution map**:

| Item | What must be recorded | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Observed tension | Actual economic outcome, actors, place/period, magnitude and stakes | 'X is important', 'few studies' |
| Non-mechanical uncertainty | What *substantive* magnitude, distribution, duration, response, or comparison is unknown beyond definitions/identities? | 'How can real output rise while carbon intensity falls?' with no further empirical uncertainty |
| Answerable question | Exact contrast the approved data, design and methods can resolve | Implicit promise to identify sectoral/technology/policy causes using only an accounting identity |
| Approved answer | Closest verified Results finding and units; evidence type (descriptive/accounting/causal) | Referring to predicted Results as already proved |
| Closest predecessor | Actual paper, finding, unit, period, identification/design, cited correctly | 'No previous research does this' without adequate literature review |
| Marginal contribution | What reader can distinguish or assess that is not already answered by the predecessor | 'We combine LMDI, Tapio and regional statistics' as novelty by itself |
| Boundary/hand-off | What Discussion may explain with local evidence versus literature only | Mechanism leap; repeated defensive caveats |

**Identity/puzzle discriminator.** A ratio such as emissions/output falls mechanically whenever emissions grow more slowly than output. Coexistence is a valid *economic motivation*, but is not in itself an unanswered mechanism question. Convert it into a substantive, identifiable empirical uncertainty: by how much did absolute emissions change; how does the strength or spread of the adjustment vary by units; how much do accounting components offset; how often do reductions persist? Other papers may pose causal or structural questions if their identification supports them. Do not devalue accurate descriptive evidence, but do not market a definition as a discovery.

**Contribution test.** Ask 'Relative to [closest verified research], which outcome, setting, observation, economically useful comparison or credibility assessment can we now characterize that they could not?' A coordinated multi-method account can contribute through a previously unavailable consistent cross-unit comparison, but only if the specific additional economic insight is stated and correctly bounded. Avoid novelty claims requiring a comprehensive priority search not performed.

**Writing structure.** Sequence concrete economic tension/stakes → bounded economic question → closest relevant antecedents → credible data/method approach → concise main answer/contribution; adapt order and length. No mandatory four-paragraph scheme or numeric-result quota. **Opening check:** the first sentence should normally name an observable economic phenomenon or consequential comparison, not merely announce “aggregate and distributional dimensions” or another conceptual label; such terms can be explained after the phenomenon. **Results-preview check:** select only the smallest set of headline numerical facts needed to answer the main question and substantiate the contribution, often one linked contrast; do not pack every city classification, robustness case, figure or Section 4 result into the closing Introduction paragraph. Do not delete a genuinely indispensable magnitude for arbitrary brevity. Respect the target journal and researcher-approved scope.

## 2. Build a relational Literature Review, not an annotated bibliography

Use the few literature streams that actually explain the paper's question. For each important source, inspect original findings to the extent available and record:

| Stream/research question | Specific study and verified finding | Outcome, unit, period, design | Agreement / difference with other studies (supported?) | Limit of transfer | Unresolved substantive comparison | Exactly what current study adds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Draft from **relations among findings** rather than 'A finds..., B finds..., C finds...'. A useful section shows established finding → valid complement/contrast → reasons grounded in differences of outcomes/geographies/periods/designs → remaining answerable uncertainty → this study's contribution. If studies are not methodologically comparable, **state the difference rather than manufacturing disagreement**. One directly relevant source can support a straightforward claim; there is no minimum citations per paragraph or paper-count quota.

**Topic discipline.** Literature Review examines what existing economic research has learned; Data/Methods defines ratios, quantile algorithms, sampling and inventory sources; Results supplies current measurements; Discussion considers mechanisms, competing interpretations, and policy implications with the right evidence status. Move lengthy CV/log-SD/LMDI definitions and source-vintage inventories out of Literature Review unless *measurement itself* is the paper's genuinely central scholarly question.

**Comparison examples (illustrative; reverify for each manuscript):**

- **Spatial disparity vs convergence:** A falling city carbon-intensity level, a decline in cross-city sigma dispersion, conditional beta convergence, and quantile-specific provincial-sector convergence are different empirical objects. Compare findings without using one as proof of another.
- **Economic expansion/decomposition:** Accounting contributions show the balance of scale and intensity within a chosen identity, not a causal technology or policy coefficient. Compare studies' boundaries and components.
- **Growth–emissions decoupling:** Distinguish output rising with absolute emissions falling from output rising faster than emissions. Annual classifications and endpoint cumulative classifications answer different economic questions.
- **Other empirical fields:** Distinguish treatment-effect identification from descriptive correlations, regression coefficients from welfare magnitude, and sector-level results from firm-specific causal claims.

**Coverage before elegance.** Verify cited work for the exact statement, including sign, scope, period, data boundary and whether only an abstract was accessed. Mark unresolved text as unverified; do not use improved prose as proof that a citation gate has passed. Scope is chosen to establish relevant economic scholarship, not to maximize citations.

## 3. Hypothesis taxonomy: a mandatory cross-paper rule

For **all future articles**, **NEVER turn a robustness or measurement-sensitivity analysis into a standalone numbered research hypothesis** such as `H4 (Measurement Sensitivity)`, `H5 (Robustness of Findings)`, or equivalent. In particular:

| Type | Correct treatment | Incorrect treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Substantive economic proposition with observable implication | Numbered Hn if author/journal uses hypotheses; or a descriptive research expectation/question | Formula-driven inevitable truth advertised as an empirical hypothesis |
| Accounting identity / arithmetic relationship | Define as accounting design or empirical decomposition question; measured factor signs are empirical observations | Identity validity as hypothesis or causal finding |
| Robustness/sensitivity to sample, year, alternative measurement, different emissions inventory, spatial allocation, deflator or model diagnostic | **Unnumbered supporting evaluation**, still fully report materially changing conclusions | `H4` or any other Hn 'Sensitivity hypothesis supported' |

There is no requirement for exactly three hypotheses. Label them to fit the paper's real substantive questions. For a purely descriptive manuscript, an explicit Research Questions section can be preferable to calling descriptive assessments causal hypotheses, subject to author approval. If robustness reveals a new substantive phenomenon requiring theory/testing, it may motivate a **distinct, genuinely economic hypothesis**; the mere existence of measurement sensitivity does not.

**Legacy migration is not an automatic edit.** If a protected paper already numbers measurement sensitivity as H4, report the specific passages and cross-references affected (Introduction, Section 2, Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusions, tables/captions as needed). Offer a minimal, author-approved migration: preserve the robustness analysis and numerical results, relabel as unnumbered 'robustness/measurement assessment', revise references and conclusions consistently. Do not silently edit the approved manuscript or delete the substantive sensitivity results. In an isolated pilot candidate explicitly authorized for comparison, demonstrate the migration without modifying the protected original.

## 4. Independent review decision — Codex × Cursor through real ACP

Cursor must independently read the manuscript, approved Results and Discussion, and the closest reference excerpts; do not accept Codex's matrices as proof. For each scope item:

1. **`INTRODUCTION_ARGUMENT`: PASS/HOLD** — Identify the actual economic question and test whether it exceeds tautology/method-combination. Validate bounded answer, verified marginal contribution, concrete opening economic phenomenon, and a selective—not overloaded—results preview.
2. **`LITERATURE_SYNTHESIS`: PASS/HOLD** — Check thematic synthesis, at least one supported relation among key findings per substantive stream, fit/mismatch, research gap and contribution; do not fabricate debate.
3. **`HYPOTHESIS_TAXONOMY`: PASS/HOLD** — No robustness/measurement `Hn` in a new candidate; legacy H4 is logged for migration with cross-section dependency mapping and researcher authority.

Return `Section/paragraph | Exact contested text | Approved manuscript/verified source | Severity (Critical/Major/Minor) | Economic impact | Bounded fix`. Explain the delta from the baseline in reader-understandable terms. These are **editorial subchecks** inside the existing economic interpretation gate. They do NOT create a new official JSON validation format or override citation integrity, QA or independent native-agent assignment requirements from other installed Skills.

## 5. Reusable release acceptance and legacy pilot comparisons

**A/B evaluation (when requested):** Use a frozen approved manuscript, identical paper type, actual Results and Discussion, same verified literature set and same no-new-model/no-new-citation rules. A = current approved Introduction/Literature. B = candidate authored under v2.3 in an isolated directory; a fair test is not guaranteed by using a few words from examples above.

**Report:** Question specificity beyond identity; closest-study contribution precision; meaningful cross-study synthesis; hypothesis taxonomy; English clarity/length; scientific fidelity; evidence-source verification status; scope changes. Provide side-by-side paragraphs and concrete examples, not subjective scores alone. Cursor audits blindly or without being told which is preferred if operationally feasible. Give **PASS** only when B is not worse on source fidelity/scientific boundaries and materially improves at least one real weakness, including removing sensitivity H4 in a *candidate* with appropriate dependency warnings.

**Do not grade the candidate by resemblance to a reference draft.** The goal is to achieve or exceed the approved manuscript's substantive economics and clarity, not copy its sentences. A valid test may find v2.8 already strong and conclude that only a small bounded change is warranted.

**Legacy RC1 pilot states (for evaluating past trials, not a mandatory release gate):** `v2.3_PILOT_PROMISING` if genuine ACP confirms a meaningful non-regressive gain; `v2.3_PILOT_NO_MATERIAL_GAIN` if source fidelity is sound but writing gains are negligible; `v2.3_PILOT_HOLD` if source drift, unsupported claims, misleading contributions, missing real ACP or invalid hypothesis taxonomy remain. **Do not equate editorial pilot success with the separate formal literature-validator PASS.**
